Private workspace User guide

User guide

Your master documents

The Resume tab holds both masters — the resume, and the cover letter underneath it. Both are read-only and shown exactly as they print. Each has its own PDF button.

They change in one direction only: you write against a real posting in Fit Score, then promote a version up. Nothing an agent writes lands here on its own — thirty applications should not slowly erode what you actually wrote.

Keywords

Every resume entry carries two lists: the job titles it is evidence for, and the skills it demonstrates. These are what the agent matches a posting against. An untagged entry is invisible to it.

✦ Tag keywords fills both with AI. It only touches entries that have none, so running it again is safe. It saves for you when it finishes.

The postings list

Across the top of the Resume tab, one row per application — it stays one line tall however many you have. It carries the same columns as Job Postings: company, job title, status, posting, deadline, sent, contact, notes and what has been built.

It is read-only. It is here so you can see where every application stands while you look at your masters; Job Postings is the one place you change any of it, so there is never a question of which copy of a row you typed into.

Built links still work — they open that application's resume or cover letter ready to print, with a chip for how many versions exist. Rows for applications you have not marked complete are dimmed.

The table starts closed, with the count on the heading — the masters underneath are what this tab is for. The Public column shows which application's resume is live on the site; publishing happens in Job Postings.

Sorting

Click any column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse it; an arrow marks the one you are on. A blank always sorts last whichever way the arrow points — an empty deadline is not an early one. Posting sorts by how many words of the description you actually have and Built by how many versions exist, rather than by the text in the cell. The two tables sort independently.

Bold, and blue

Anywhere you write document text, **stars** makes it bold and ***three*** makes it bold and blue. Headings, your name and employer names already print blue. The markers never reach a PDF, the frozen record, or the agent — they are stripped from everything but the rendered page.

Job Postings

Every posting on one row, every field its own column — company, job title, status, how much of the description you actually have, deadline, sent date, contact, notes, and what has been built. Click a row to open it in full underneath.

Sorting

Click any column heading to sort by it, and again to reverse it; an arrow marks the one you are on. A blank always sorts last whichever way the arrow points — an empty deadline is not an early one. Posting sorts by how many words of the description you actually have and Built by how many versions exist, rather than by the text in the cell. The two tables sort independently.

Editing in the row

Deadline, Sent and Contact email are yours to type into right in the row. Notes shows a one-line preview — click it and a full-size, scrollable box opens, so an interview log or an email trail fits. Everything saves as you type. Clicking anywhere else on the row opens that posting below.

The public resume

Publish in the Public column puts that application's resume on jessicadougherty.com — it becomes what both Resume buttons on the landing page show, and anyone can read it. The row that is live says so, with a link to open it. Publishing another replaces it; it takes effect in seconds, with no deploy.

It publishes a copy. Editing that version afterwards changes nothing on the site until you publish again. Until you publish anything at all, the site shows the resume page that ships with it.

Removing applications

Each row starts with a checkbox, and the one in the header selects or deselects every row at once. Remove, at the top of the box, counts what you have ticked and hides those applications on click.

It is the same soft delete as the Delete button on an open posting: the data stays in your repo. Tick show deleted and they come straight back.

Posting tells you whether you have the real description or only an alert teaser. Nothing can be generated from a teaser.

The table starts closed, with the count on the heading; the posting you are working on opens underneath it.

Adding a posting

Add posting for a blank one, or One-click capture to grab it from a job page with a bookmarklet — see Capture below.

Generating

Generate resume + letter reads the posting, picks entries from your master by keyword, writes a tailored summary, and drafts the letter. It writes only into this application.

Statuses, and what freezing means

Three statuses on the application, and again on the resume and the letter: draft, complete, deleted.

  • Draft stays live — edits to your masters flow through
  • Complete freezes the text. That frozen copy is the record of what the employer received, and editing your master afterwards cannot change it
  • Deleted hides it. The data stays in your repo; tick show deleted to bring it back
Moving a completed document back to draft asks first, because it discards the record of what you sent.

Fit score

The Application box holds three columns: the posting on the left, then the resume and the cover letter written against it, so you can read a requirement and its answer without scrolling between them. The posting's heading is the picker — changing it switches every column to that application. Show opens the description for editing; edits save to that application.

Every requirement in the posting is scored twice — once against the resume, once against the cover letter — because they fail in different ways. A resume can cover something the letter never mentions.

  • Copy fix on the resume side hands you the suggestion. There is no single unambiguous place to apply it, so it does not guess.
  • Add to letter appends the suggestion to this application's letter as a draft paragraph for you to rewrite.

Revising, and approving

Copy fix hands you the resume suggestion on the clipboard and changes nothing — there is no single unambiguous place to apply it. Add to letter appends the letter suggestion to this application's letter, never to your master.

For a full pass, don't use those: Approve the agent's questions, then Revise. Revise folds in every approved answer and every fix at once, and comes back with a proposed version you can edit — the summary, each letter paragraph, and which entries go in, with the resume rebuilding underneath as you change it.

Nothing is a version until you press Approve. Until then the current version is untouched, and Discard throws the draft away without spending your approved answers. Once approved it becomes the current version, and the Resume and Cover letter links in both Postings tables follow it.

Then score again: the Versions list shows each version's own score, so you can see whether the rewrite actually moved the number.

Everything is closed until you open it

The proposed version, score history, the questions, the versions and the ranking all start collapsed, with their counts on the headings. A draft arrives closed too — Approve, Discard and AI help sit on its heading, so you can act on one without opening it. Picking an application moves all of them — its documents, its questions, its versions, its scores — so nothing on the page is ever describing a different company.

A zero with "no evidence" is the honest answer, not a failure of the scorer. If the posting wants something you have never done, no wording fixes that — and the fix will say so rather than help you claim it.

Every run is saved to the score history, so you can see whether a rewrite actually moved the number.

One-click capture

Grabs a posting from the page you are looking at, with no copy-paste.

  1. Show your bookmarks bar: Ctrl + Shift + B
  2. Archive tab → One-click capture
  3. Drag the green button up onto your bookmarks bar — do not click it here
  4. Open any job posting and click the bookmark. It arrives as a new application.

It reads the page your browser already loaded and passes the text in the URL fragment, which never reaches any server. Nothing is scraped, which is why it works on sites that block automated fetching.

Interview prep

For after the documents are sent, when the question is whether you can stand behind them.

Pick the position at the top — the list carries the company, the title and the date it came in, and a tick against the ones you have already prepped, so two openings at one company a month apart are not the same line.

The posting, the resume and the cover letter come over side by side. The posting is editable here, because a recruiter sends a fuller description and this is where you are sitting. The two documents are not: a resume is built from the entries and summary of a version, and a completed one is frozen as the record of what you sent. Edit in Fit Score on either takes you there with the same position already selected.

What drafting gives you

Draft interview prep reads all three together and returns three things.

  • Likely questions — the question, why they will ask it, what it is really testing, and your answer. The answer is the only editable thing, and you Approve or Skip each one.
  • Double-check — where the three documents disagree, quoted. The wrong company in your own letter is better found here than across a table.
  • What the posting suggests — read-only. What was sent is the record; act on it by building a new version in Fit Score.

Re-running, and getting it back

Each section has its own Re-run, with the time it last came back beside it. Re-running the questions replaces the questions and the answers you wrote against them; re-running the others leaves the questions alone. Redraft all at the top replaces everything. Each says what it is about to replace before it does it.

Nothing a re-run replaces is thrown away. The prep as it stood is filed under Prep versions first — so is a Clear — with how many questions and how many you had answered. Restore puts one back and files the current one in its place, so you can go either way. Delete is the only thing that removes one for good, and it says so.

Questions to ask them

The other half of an interview. These are yours to say, so the wording is editable and + your own adds a blank one. What a bad answer sounds like is written next to each — that is the part you cannot look up.

AI help on an answer

Same as on the agent's questions: it drafts into a review box with its sources, nothing lands in your answer until you press Use this, and anything it could not source from your record is listed as something to confirm rather than a sentence to sign.

All of it is stored against that position, so coming back a week later brings back what you wrote.

When something breaks

"Your data repo is not connected"Environment variables missing. Vercel → Deployments → ⋯ → Redeploy.
"GitHub rejected the token (401)"Token expired — they last a year. Make a new one, update GITHUB_DATA_TOKEN, redeploy.
"lacks Contents: Read and write"Token permission. Edit it → Repository permissions → Contents → Read and write.
"Changed somewhere else"Two tabs open. Reload the one you care about least, then redo the edit.
"No Anthropic API key"Only the AI buttons need it. Add ANTHROPIC_API_KEY in Vercel and redeploy.
Bounced to loginSession expired. Normal — sign in again.

Anything not on this list, or an error that doesn't name its own fix: send me the exact wording.
